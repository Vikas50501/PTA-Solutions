Newborn & Infant Hearing Assessment

Clinic: PTA SOLUTIONS•Location: Chembur Clinic, Mumbai•Testing: OAE & BERA / ABR

Gentle Hearing Assessment for Babies & Infants

Hearing screening and diagnostic testing for babies, using appropriate tests such as OAE or ABR/BERA when available and clinically indicated. The appropriate test depends on the baby's age, history and screening results.

Early identification of hearing differences provides families with the opportunity to take timely, well-informed steps during the crucial early stages of speech and communication development. Assessments are conducted gently in a quiet, reassuring environment.

Assessment Methods

Screening & Diagnostic Technologies

D

Otoacoustic Emissions (OAE) Test

Inner Ear & Hair Cell Response

OAE testing checks for sound responses produced by the inner ear, particularly the outer hair cells. It is commonly used in newborn hearing screening and may also be used as part of an audiological assessment.

Who may benefit?

Newborns, infants and other patients when OAE testing is clinically indicated.

What to expect:

A small probe is placed in the ear canal to deliver sounds and record the ear's response. The test is generally painless.

Results:

A screening result does not by itself provide a complete picture of hearing ability. If a result is not as expected, further testing may be needed.

E

BERA / ABR Hearing Assessment

Auditory Brainstem Response

BERA, also called ABR, is an objective hearing assessment that records electrical responses from the auditory pathway in response to sound. It can help estimate hearing sensitivity and assess the auditory pathway, depending on the test protocol.

Who may benefit?

Babies and children who need further hearing assessment, or other patients for whom objective testing is indicated.

What to expect:

Small electrodes are placed on the skin and sounds are presented through earphones or suitable transducers. The patient needs to remain sufficiently still; the preparation and testing requirements vary with age and clinical circumstances.

Results:

The clinician interprets the recorded responses and explains whether additional tests or referral are needed.
