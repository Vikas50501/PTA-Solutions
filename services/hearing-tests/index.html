Hearing Tests & Audiology

Clinic: PTA SOLUTIONS•Location: Chembur Clinic, Mumbai•Standards: Sound-Treated Testing Room

Diagnostic Hearing Assessments at PTA Solutions

Hearing tests to assess hearing ability, along with middle-ear testing when indicated. We explain the results and discuss suitable next steps, including hearing-aid options when appropriate.

Many people live with gradually worsening hearing without realizing it — difficulty following conversations in noisy rooms, turning the TV volume up, or frequently asking others to repeat themselves are common early signs. An accurate diagnostic assessment gives you a clear, objective picture of your hearing health.

Clinical Protocols

Diagnostic Hearing Tests & Procedures

Explore our clinical assessment tests to understand what each involves, who may benefit, and what to expect.

A

Pure Tone Audiometry (PTA)

Air & Bone Conduction Assessment

PTA is a hearing test that measures the quietest sounds a person can hear at different pitches. The results help assess the degree and pattern of hearing loss and whether hearing sensitivity differs between the ears.

Who may benefit?

People who have difficulty hearing conversations, frequently ask others to repeat themselves, turn up the television volume, or are referred for a hearing assessment.

What to expect:

The patient listens to sounds through headphones or earphones and responds when each sound is heard. Bone-conduction testing may also be performed when indicated.

Results:

The findings are plotted on an audiogram and explained to the patient. Further assessment or medical referral may be recommended when appropriate.

B

Tympanometry

Middle-Ear Function Test

Tympanometry assesses how the eardrum moves in response to changes in air pressure in the ear canal. It helps evaluate the function of the eardrum and middle-ear system.

Who may benefit?

People with ear fullness, suspected middle-ear problems, certain hearing difficulties, or a need for further assessment alongside a hearing test.

What to expect:

A small soft probe is placed at the entrance of the ear canal. The equipment changes the air pressure and measures the response. The test is usually brief.

Results:

The findings help the clinician assess middle-ear function. They are interpreted alongside the patient's symptoms and other examination or test results.

C

Speech Audiometry (When Indicated)

Speech Recognition Testing

Speech audiometry assesses how well a person detects or recognises spoken words at suitable listening levels. It can provide additional information about functional hearing and help interpret hearing-test findings.

Who may benefit?

People who can hear sounds but struggle to understand conversations, particularly in noisy environments, or when additional speech-based information is needed.

What to expect:

The patient listens to speech or word material and responds according to the test procedure.

Results:

The findings are considered with the pure-tone audiogram and the patient's reported listening difficulties.

D

Otoacoustic Emissions (OAE) Test

Inner Ear & Hair Cell Response

OAE testing checks for sound responses produced by the inner ear, particularly the outer hair cells. It is commonly used in newborn hearing screening and may also be used as part of an audiological assessment.

Who may benefit?

Newborns, infants and other patients when OAE testing is clinically indicated.

What to expect:

A small probe is placed in the ear canal to deliver sounds and record the ear's response. The test is generally painless.

Results:

A screening result does not by itself provide a complete picture of hearing ability. If a result is not as expected, further testing may be needed.

E

BERA / ABR Hearing Assessment

Auditory Brainstem Response

BERA, also called ABR, is an objective hearing assessment that records electrical responses from the auditory pathway in response to sound. It can help estimate hearing sensitivity and assess the auditory pathway, depending on the test protocol.

Who may benefit?

Babies and children who need further hearing assessment, or other patients for whom objective testing is indicated.

What to expect:

Small electrodes are placed on the skin and sounds are presented through earphones or suitable transducers. The patient needs to remain sufficiently still; the preparation and testing requirements vary with age and clinical circumstances.

Results:

The clinician interprets the recorded responses and explains whether additional tests or referral are needed.
